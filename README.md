
Typhoon Dolphin (2026): A Marathon Storm Across the Pacific — Interactive Simulator Inside

---

In the summer of 2026, a real monster appeared over the western North Pacific.

Its name was **Dolphin** (known in Chinese as “Baihaitun” / 白海豚).

It formed near the International Date Line — almost in the middle of the Pacific. Then it raced westward for more than 6,000 kilometers before finally making landfall in Zhejiang, China.

A typical typhoon lasts about 5 to 7 days. Dolphin lived for more than 15 days.

Within 48 hours of formation it exploded into a super typhoon. At its peak, sustained winds reached 65 m/s (over 17 on the Chinese scale), and the central pressure dropped to around 910 hPa. During its strongest phase, the radius of gale-force winds exceeded 420 km, and the entire circulation stretched nearly 1,300 km across — large enough to cover several Zhejiang provinces.

Even more remarkable: it is one of the easternmost-originating storms on record that still managed to reach China at tropical-storm strength or higher.

Most people only remember the torrential rain and strong winds on the day it landed. Few have ever seen the complete journey from birth to death.

So I built an interactive **Typhoon Dolphin Full Lifecycle Simulator**.

### What can you see in the simulator?

When you open the page, a simplified map of the western North Pacific appears.

Click “Play” and Dolphin slowly emerges near the Date Line, then steadily moves west.

You can watch in real time:

- Its current center position (latitude & longitude)
- Central pressure and maximum sustained wind
- Intensity category (tropical storm → typhoon → severe typhoon → super typhoon)
- A color-coded intensity bar that shifts from green (tropical storm) to orange (typhoon) to red (severe/super typhoon)

You can drag the progress bar freely. Want to see the peak intensity? Jump to the middle. Want the exact moment of landfall? Drag to the later stages.

Below the map is a complete timeline of key events:

- July 27: Formed near the International Date Line
- Rapid intensification to 65 m/s peak intensity
- Multiple eyewall replacement cycles with intensity fluctuations
- August 9: Made landfall in Yuhuan, Zhejiang, followed by a second landfall in Yueqing
- Later moved inland and gradually dissipated

Each milestone comes with a short explanation so you know exactly what was happening at that moment.

### Why is this worth watching?

Because Dolphin was truly special.

Most typhoons form east of the Philippines or near the Mariana Islands and follow relatively familiar tracks. Dolphin started near the Date Line and ran a marathon across the entire western North Pacific.

Its lifespan was more than three times that of an average typhoon, its track exceeded 6,000 km, and its circulation was unusually large. The combination of extreme longevity, long-distance travel, and high intensity is extremely rare in the historical record.

After watching the simulator, you will better understand:

- Why it produced widespread extreme rainfall across East China
- Why track forecasts had greater uncertainty in the early stages
- Why the remnant circulation continued to affect Henan, Hubei, and even areas farther north after landfall

Weather is not just dry numbers — it is a real, dramatic process that crossed half the Pacific Ocean.
